## Findings

### Bunny deletes an upload that was cut off, even after a newer one landed (S5, ruling Q55)

Live probes (`crates/httporter-bunny/tests/live.rs`) showed that when Bunny Storage ends an upload that was cut off part way, it deletes the path, including a newer upload of it that was already answered 201. It ends the cut-off one at once when the client closes, or about 60 s after the last bytes when the connection just goes quiet (9 of 9 trials). After a kill, the resume re-sent the cut-off uploads within 1–11 s, before Bunny had ended the old ones, so 2–3 files vanished per run.

Fix: on Bunny (`Capabilities::cut_uploads_delete_late`), every upload is marked in the journal before it goes. An upload sent again with that mark (after a crash, a pause, or a network error inside the same attempt) is looked for in its folder's listing 180 s after its answer and sent again if it is gone (up to three times, then it fails with a clear reason). Uploads never interrupted are not checked. Live S5 runs after the fix: every file on the zone; the checks found 3 and 2 files missing after their 201 and re-sent them. Details in `docs/notes/bunny-behavior.md`.

### Bunny upload speed (S1)

With all 32 slots busy the whole run, most uploads take 0.2–0.3 s, but p90 is ~4.5 s and single calls take 16–65 s with no error. The rate is about 32 ÷ the mean call time, so Bunny's slow tail sets it. Bunny's rate also fell over the day for every client: the harness measured 52 → 43 → 18 files/s over ~2 h, and the user's Python uploader (`bunny_upload.py`, 32 workers, no checksum) managed **15.7 files/s** on the same line right after, against ~115 files/s on 2026-10-29. Same hour, HTTPorter was at or above the script. The 94 files/s target came from the script's good-day rate; it is Bunny-side and not met on 2026-11-04. Raising *Simultaneous transfers* in Settings (Bunny allows up to 96) is the lever when Bunny's tail is long.

### Recursive delete on Bunny (ruling Q54)

Bunny's recursive folder DELETE runs at ~8 files/s and answers only when finished (13,793 files ≈ 30 min, beyond any request timeout). A folder delete now queues one job per file, folder last: 94.5 files/s.
